2. **The action was taken inside a Special Economic Zone and inside a Unit, and Section 22 of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 therefore governs it.** Section 22 provides that no investigation, inspection, search or seizure shall be carried out in a Special Economic Zone or in a Unit by any agency or officer other than those specified under sub-section (1) of Section 20 or sub-section (1) of Section 21, **without prior intimation or approval of the Development Commissioner**; and that where any investigation, inspection, search or seizure is to be carried out in a Unit, it shall be carried out by the authorised officer or agency, as the case may be, **with the prior intimation of the Development Commissioner**. The **first proviso** to Section 22 further provides that no such investigation, inspection, search or seizure shall be carried out in a Unit by any agency or officer other than those referred to in sub-section (1) of Section 20 or sub-section (1) of Section 21 **without prior approval of the Development Commissioner concerned**. The provision is thus in two limbs, both of which are conditions precedent: **(a) prior intimation to, or in the case of any other agency or officer, prior approval of, the Development Commissioner; and (b) reasons recorded in writing** that a notified offence has been, or is likely to be, committed.

3. **The same two conditions are imposed by the notification under which alone the Customs formation can act inside the Zone.** By S.O. 2667(E) dated 05.08.2016, issued by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, Department of Commerce, in exercise of the powers conferred by Section 22 of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005, the jurisdictional Commissioner of Customs has been specified as the agency for the purposes of carrying out investigation, inspection, search or seizure in a Special Economic Zone or in a Unit, subject expressly to the conditions that the same shall be carried out **for reasons to be recorded in writing** and **with prior intimation to the Development Commissioner concerned**. The specification is of the **jurisdictional Commissioner of Customs**, and the power is conferred subject to those conditions; it is not a general licence to any officer of any Commissionerate to enter a Unit inside the Zone at will.

4. **The judgment on which the Department itself relies makes compliance the very test.** In *RHC Global Exports Private Limited v. Union of India*, Special Civil Application No. 5978 of 2023, decided on 06.06.2023, the Hon'ble High Court of Gujarat declined to interfere with a search and seizure carried out inside a Special Economic Zone unit precisely because the officers were an agency specified under the notification dated 05.08.2016 **and because the Development Commissioner had in fact been informed** of the action. The authority is therefore not authority for the proposition that Customs may enter a Unit as it pleases; it is authority for the proposition that the entry stands or falls on the specification and on the intimation. Where, as here, neither the specification of the acting officers nor the prior intimation to, or approval of, the Development Commissioner is shown, and no reasons in writing are produced, the action falls outside Section 22 and is without jurisdiction.

(c) the officers who entered the Unit did not show, and have not since disclosed, any authorisation of the jurisdictional Commissioner of Customs, or any specification under Section 20(1) or Section 21(1) of the said Act; and
(d) our Letter of Approval is endorsed to the Specified Officer and to the Authorised Officer, NDR Infrastructure FTWZ, and to no other formation.
