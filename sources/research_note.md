## SEZ/FTWZ units, Z-type Bills of Entry, and the powers of SIIB over FTWZ/SEZ cargo
### Prepared for: Aaapurti Renewable Energy India Pvt. Ltd. — proposed W.P. (Madras High Court)
### B/E No. 2875354 dated 01.08.2026 — Blister Copper, CTH 7402 — detained at ACC, Chennai by SIIB

## PART A — LOCATION OF THE GOODS: CONFIRMED, AND WHAT IT MEANS

**Confirmed position (client instruction):** the consignment has at all material times been lying at the **Air Cargo Complex, Meenambakkam**, and the sampling on 07.08.2026 was also carried out **at the airport**. Nothing complained of happened inside the MEPZ SEZ or inside the Petitioner's FTWZ unit.

### Consequence: the SEZ Act s.22 jurisdiction ground is dropped

Section 22 of the SEZ Act is drafted **territorially** — it regulates "investigation, inspection, search or seizure ... **in** a Special Economic Zone or in a Unit", and S.O. 2666(E)/2667(E) likewise authorise the named officers to act "within a Special Economic Zone or a Unit". The protection attaches to the **place where the officer acts**, not to the **destination of the cargo**. A Z-type Bill of Entry proves where the goods were *going*; it does not carry the SEZ Act's territorial shield backwards into the customs area of an airport.

While the goods lie in the customs area of a customs station they are in ordinary customs territory; SIIB is a formation of the jurisdictional Customs Commissionerate and its officers are "proper officers" within s.2(34) of the Customs Act. A plea that "SIIB has no jurisdiction over Z-type cargo" would therefore be met with two words — *where were the goods?* — and would fail. **The s.22 / S.O. 2666(E) / S.O. 2667(E) ground, and the Rule 47(5) rebuttal that existed only to answer it, have accordingly been deleted from the petition.** Part B below is retained for reference only, in case the Department itself invokes the 2016 scheme.

### The case as it now stands (all of it independent of location)

1. **No power of indefinite detention.** The Customs Act has no power of "detention" de hors s.110. Either seize and record/communicate the grounds, or let the goods go. An oral hold is an executive act without statutory sanction.
2. **Seizure without the safeguards of a seizure.** Every practical consequence of s.110 has been achieved while denying the s.110(2) six-month discipline, the grounds of belief and the s.110A provisional-release right. *Jatinder Kumar Sachdeva* (Del., 08.12.2016): **there is no provision for detention in the Act** and the Department cannot, **"under the garb of detention"**, escape the consequences of a seizure. *Exim Incorporation* (Bom., DB, 09.12.2020): the **first illegality** is detaining without effecting a seizure. *Jatin Ahuja* (SC, 11.09.2025) on the s.110(2) discipline.
3. **SIIB has no power to undo an assessed Z-BoE.** The assessed/registered Z-type Bill of Entry, endorsed by the **Authorised Officer**, **is itself the permission to transfer the goods into the Zone** (Rules 27(10) and 75; and post-2016, Rule 47(5) places assessment/re-assessment/adjudication for a Unit with the jurisdictional Customs authority). Power to disturb an assessment is conferred **only** on the **proper officer** (s.2(34)) under **s.17(4)/(5)** (speaking order) or **s.18**, and power to withhold goods only under **s.110**. SIIB is an intelligence/investigation wing: it did not assess this B/E and has **no power to cancel, review, re-open or render inoperative** an assessment made by the Authorised Officer. None of s.17(4)/(5), s.18 or s.110 was invoked — so there is **no source of power** for what was done. *Note the pleading limit: this is a 'no power to un-assess / no power to detain' plea, NOT the discarded 'SIIB has no jurisdiction over FTWZ cargo' plea.*
4. **Sections 53(1) and 51.** The SEZ is deemed territory outside the customs territory for authorised operations and the SEZ Act overrides inconsistent law; the goods are moving into an FTWZ under the warehousing procedure, not into the DTA, so no question of duty or home-consumption clearance arises. *Morgan Tectronics* (CESTAT, persuasive only) treated the SEZ Customs authority, not Air Cargo Customs, as competent over SEZ-destined cargo.
5. **Sampling without a mahazar.** No panchanama/mahazar, no sampling or test memo, no copy supplied — ss.100–105 r/w s.110 and natural justice; such samples have no evidentiary value.
6. **Breach of binding CBIC circulars.** 84/95-Cus, 01/2011-Cus, 30/2013-Cus, 30/2017-Cus and 43/2017-Cus (see Part B-7).
7. **Article 14 and Article 300A.** Eighteen identical consignments cleared between 25.11.2025 and 01.08.2026, including one on the very same day; property worth Rs. 8,32,51,039.90 withheld without authority of law.
8. **The Department's own CHL report** (Lab Nos. 9319–9324-MEPZ dated 06.02.2026, on SIIB's own samples) already answers the question now reopened.

## PART B — NOTIFICATIONS AND CIRCULARS (verified against primary text; B-1 to B-6 retained for reference only)

### 1. S.O. 2665(E) dated 05.08.2016 — Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Dept. of Commerce), F.No. C.1/1/2009-SEZ
Issued under **s.21(1), SEZ Act, 2005**. Notifies the following as "notified offences" under the SEZ Act:

**Customs Act, 1962:** ss. 28, 28AA and 28AAA; ss. 74 and 75; s. 111; s. 113; s. 115; s. 124; s. 135; s. 104.
(Also ss. 9, 9AA, 11/11A/11AA of the Central Excise Act, 1944 and ss. 73/73A/73B/75, 76, 89, 91 of the Finance Act, 1994.)

> **Use for us:** s.110 (seizure) is **NOT** in the list. Nor is s.17, s.18, s.48 or s.144. The notified-offence gateway is therefore confined to confiscation/demand/prosecution provisions. A mere doubt about metal composition, with no allegation of mis-declaration attracting s.111, does not engage a notified offence at all.

### 2. S.O. 2666(E) dated 05.08.2016 — the enforcement-officer notification
Issued under **s.21(2) and the second proviso to s.22**. Verbatim effect:

> "...the Central Government hereby authorises the **Additional Director General, Directorate of Revenue Intelligence** for offences under the Customs Act, 1962 ... to be the enforcement officer(s) in respect of any notified offence or offences committed or likely to be committed in a Special Economic Zone. The enforcement officer(s), **for the reasons to be recorded in writing**, may carry out the investigation, inspection, search or seizure in the Special Economic Zone or Unit and **shall intimate the details of any action initiated** under sub-section (3) of section 21 of the Act **to the Joint Secretary in charge of Special Economic Zones Division in the Department of Commerce immediately and in any case not later than seven days** of initiation of any action."

> **Use for us:** the only Customs officer named is the **ADG, DRI** — expressly and exhaustively. The Additional Commissioner of Customs, SIIB, Air Cargo Complex is **not** an authorised enforcement officer under this notification. Even the ADG, DRI must (a) record reasons in writing and (b) report to the JS (SEZ) within 7 days.

### 3. S.O. 2667(E) dated 05.08.2016 — the jurisdictional-Commissioner notification
Issued under **s.22**. Verbatim effect:

> "...the Central Government authorises the **jurisdictional Customs Commissioner** ... for the reasons to be recorded in writing, to carry out the investigation, inspection, search or seizure in a Special Economic Zone or Unit **with prior intimation to the Development Commissioner, concerned**."

> **Use for us:** this is the *only* route by which ordinary Customs formations can act inside an SEZ/FTWZ, and it is conditioned on (a) the **Commissioner** (not an Additional Commissioner or an Inspector/Superintendent of SIIB), (b) **written reasons**, and (c) **prior intimation to the Development Commissioner**. This is precisely why R4 (Development Commissioner, MEPZ) is a necessary party — the record of intimation, if any, lies with him.

### 4. SEZ Act, 2005 — s.21 and s.22 (statutory text)
- **s.21(1):** Central Govt. may notify any act/omission punishable under any Central Act as a "notified offence".
- **s.21(2):** Central Govt. may authorise "any officer or agency" to be the enforcement officer/agency for notified offences committed in an SEZ.
- **s.21(3):** the authorised officer/agency has the corresponding powers of investigation, inspection, search or seizure under the relevant Central Act.
- **s.22:** the agency/officer specified under s.20 or s.21 may, **with prior intimation to the Development Commissioner concerned**, carry out investigation/inspection/search/seizure in an SEZ or a Unit **if such agency or officer has reasons to believe (reasons to be recorded in writing) that a notified offence has been committed or is likely to be committed**.
  - **First proviso:** no investigation/inspection/search/seizure shall be carried out in an SEZ **by any agency or officer other than those referred to in s.21(2)/(3) without the prior approval of the Development Commissioner concerned**.
  - **Second proviso:** an officer/agency so authorised by the Central Govt. may act **without** prior intimation/approval of the Development Commissioner. (This is the peg for S.O. 2666(E), and it covers only the ADG, DRI.)
- **s.51:** overriding effect over any other inconsistent law.
- **s.53(1):** an SEZ is **deemed to be a territory outside the customs territory of India** for authorised operations.

### 5. G.S.R. 772(E) dated 08.08.2016 — inserted **Rule 47(5)**, SEZ Rules, 2006
Confers powers of **demand, refund, assessment/reassessment and adjudication** on the **jurisdictional Customs/Central Excise authorities** (previously with the Development Commissioner/Specified Officer). Operationalised by **CBIC Circular No. 11/2017-Cus dated 31.03.2017**.

> **Use for us — and the limit of it:** this is the Department's best answer to a jurisdictional plea. Note carefully that Rule 47(5) is about **demand/refund/adjudication**, not about **detention, search, seizure or sampling**, which remain governed by s.22 and S.O. 2666/2667(E). Keep this distinction sharp; do not let it be blurred.

### 6. SEZ Rules, 2006 — the Z-type Bill of Entry
- **Rule 29 (Procedure for Import), r/w sub-rule (2):** imports into an SEZ require a **Bill of Entry for home consumption with a special endorsement**, assessed by the **Authorised Officer**; the **registered/assessed Bill of Entry so endorsed is itself the permission for transfer of the goods to the SEZ**. Sealed FCL containers are admitted on seal verification; LCL cargo on verification of marks and numbers at entry or at the Unit; the **fifth copy of the Bill of Entry acknowledging receipt completes the customs out-of-charge formalities**.
- **Rule 27(10) and Rule 75:** goods are **assessed and allowed inward/outward movement on the basis of self-declaration** by the SEZ/FTWZ unit.
- **Rule 18(5) (2nd proviso):** FTWZ units may hold goods on behalf of foreign/DTA suppliers and buyers, and may re-sell, re-invoice or re-export imported goods; units are shown as importer along with the client in the inbound Bill of Entry.

> **Use for us — this is the strongest structural point.** Once the Authorised Officer assessed and endorsed the Z-type Bill of Entry, that endorsement **is, by force of Rule 29(2), the statutory permission to move the goods into the FTWZ**. An oral, unwritten "hold" by SIIB does not and cannot nullify a subsisting statutory permission granted by a different statutory authority. If the Department wants to undo it, it must either reassess under s.17(4)/s.18 or seize under s.110 — both of which require a **written, reasoned order**.

### 6A. The "Z-type" Bill of Entry — what actually creates it (verified)
**There is no notification that separately prescribes a "Z-type Bill of Entry".** "Z" is a *document-type code* in ICEGATE/ICES, not a statutory category. The chain is:

1. **Rule 29, SEZ Rules, 2006** — the substantive procedure for import into an SEZ/FTWZ: a Bill of Entry for home consumption *with a special endorsement*, assessed by the **Authorised Officer**; the assessed/endorsed BoE **is itself the permission** to transfer the goods to the Zone.
2. **Notification No. 36/2018-Customs (N.T.) dated 11.05.2018 (G.S.R. 447(E))** — Bill of Entry (Electronic Integrated Declaration and Paperless Processing) Regulations, 2018, made under **s.157 r/w ss.46 and 47** of the Customs Act and in supersession of the 2011 Regulations; the BoE is **deemed filed and self-assessment completed** on entry of the electronic integrated declaration on the Customs Automated System.
3. **Ministry of Commerce & Industry, Department of Commerce (SEZ Division), letter No. J.16/3/2007-SEZ Vol-V dated 22.06.2024** (on DG (Systems), CBIC e-mail dated 19.06.2024) — filing for **all non-IT/ITES SEZs and FTWZs moves to ICEGATE w.e.f. 01.07.2024**; no further filing on the NSDL SEZ-Online system; a model Public Notice is annexed ("Roll out of Indian Customs EDI System — ICEGATE for Imports and Exports at SEZs"). Circulated to trade by **EPCES Circular No. 469 dated 24.06.2024**.
4. **Clarification of even number dated 29.06.2024** — transition rules; records expressly that *"the 'T' type B/E in ICEGATE meant for DTA clearance from trading/warehousing unit cannot be filed unless there is a corresponding 'Z' type B/E, such details being mandatory."*
5. **ICEGATE document-type codes:** **Z** = import into SEZ/FTWZ; **T** = DTA sale (trading/warehousing unit); **M** = DTA sale (manufacturing unit); **V** = inter-SEZ transfer (Rule 30(15)).
6. **Movement from the gateway/ACC into the Zone** runs on the transhipment/EDI procedure notified after SEZ-Online–ICES integration (each Commissionerate issues its own Public Notice; Chennai ACC has one — **to be obtained**, as it identifies the officer competent to permit movement of SEZ cargo out of ACC, which is *not* SIIB).

> **Use for us:** plead the source as **Rule 29(2) + Notf. 36/2018-Cus (N.T.) + DoC letter dated 22.06.2024 (and clarification dated 29.06.2024)** — never as a "Z-type notification", which does not exist. The decisive point is negative: **none** of these instruments confers on any officer a power to withhold, suspend or keep in abeyance an already-assessed Z-type BoE, or to stop the movement of the goods under it. That is the backbone of Ground 3.

### 7. CBIC circulars on detention, sampling and re-test
- **Circular No. 84/95-Cus** ("Customs detention of imported cargo at ports"): Board notes DRI "insisting upon detention of imported cargo till such time as the enquiry/investigation undertaken by it is completed"; directs that **long detentions of imported cargo are to be avoided by Customs authorities including Intelligence and enforcement authorities**, and that where long detention is unavoidable the cargo should be **moved to a customs warehouse under s.49**.
- **Circular No. 01/2011-Cus dated 04.01.2011**, para 4: *"Seizure should be resorted to only when the Customs officers have a reason to believe that the goods in question are liable to confiscation ... However, there may be situations when the goods are to be detained for purpose of tests etc. to confirm the declaration. In such cases the endeavour should be to **quickly undertake the necessary action (test/enquiry etc.)** and take appropriate legal action thereafter so that the **period of detention is kept to the minimum**."*
- **Circular No. 30/2013-Cus:** reiterates 01/2011 and adds that **continued detention in excess of three days must be brought to the notice of the Commissioner of Customs**; "there can be no justification to hold up consignments for long periods"; **"any deviation or lapse shall be proceeded against by the Board."**
- **Circular No. 30/2017-Cus** (giving effect to WTO TFA Arts. 5.3.1/5.3.3): **all test reports, adverse or otherwise, must be communicated to the importer immediately on receipt**; a re-test may be sought within 10 days; and **where fresh samples have to be drawn, "such sampling should be done in the presence of the importer or his representative."**
- **Circular No. 43/2017-Cus:** where CRCL cannot test an item, samples go to a listed/empanelled laboratory; **standard sampling technique of the laboratory must be followed** and sampling requirements ascertained with the lab beforehand; where the lab takes more than three days, escalation is contemplated.

> **Use for us:** the 07.08.2026 sampling was (on our instructions) done **without a mahazar, without a sampling memo, and without the Petitioner's presence/attestation**, and no test report has been communicated. That is a direct breach of Circular 30/2017 and 43/2017; and the continuing hold breaches 01/2011, 30/2013 and 84/95. These circulars **bind the Department** (*Paper Products Ltd. v. CCE*, (1999) 7 SCC 84 — Board circulars are binding on Revenue; verify before citation).

## PART C — CASE LAW

### C-1. Authorities that support us

#### The core proposition: there is no power of "detention" in the Customs Act — verified authorities

**A. *Union of India v. Jatin Ahuja*** — **Supreme Court**, Civil Appeal No. 3489 of 2024, judgment dated **11.09.2025**. Held that the *only* power to extend time is that under the **first proviso to s.110(2)**, and that where no show cause notice is issued within the prescribed period the goods must be returned; the Court declined to follow the contrary Bombay view in *Jayant Hansraj Shah*. Quoted and applied by the Delhi High Court in W.P.(C) 14749/2025 (08.10.2025) and W.P.(C) 16869/2025 (07.11.2025). **Binding. Our strongest authority on the s.110 discipline.**

**B. *Jatinder Kumar Sachdeva v. Union of India*** — **Delhi High Court**, W.P.(C) No. 1492 of 2016, Sanjeev Sachdeva J., **08.12.2016**, reported [2017] 77 taxmann.com 50. The Department argued the goods had not been seized but "merely detained for clearance", so s.110(2) did not apply. Rejected: **"there is no provision for detention of goods in the Act"** and the Department **"under the garb of detention, could not avoid the consequences flowing from seizure"**. Release directed, without prejudice to lawful action. **This is the precise answer to the defence the Department will run here.**

**C. *Exim Incorporation v. Union of India*** — **Bombay High Court**, W.P. No. 1898 of 2020, Ujjal Bhuyan & Abhay Ahuja JJ., reserved 24.11.2020, **pronounced 09.12.2020**; 2020 (12) TMI 329. Bills of entry filed May 2019; duty paid; clearances given; goods nonetheless held at Nhava Sheva on the instructions of **DRI**, which insisted on 100% examination in the proprietor's presence, and a s.49 warehousing request was ignored. Held: **the first illegality is that the authorities detained the goods without effecting a seizure**, and the second that the time limit was exceeded even treating it as a seizure; release directed within two weeks, with a caution against misuse of power. Referred to *Om Udyog*, *Mohd. Salman Khan*, *Sai Incorporation*, *Shiv Shakti Trading* and *Ramnarain Bishwanath*. **Closest factual parallel to our case (investigating agency instructing the port formation to hold assessed cargo, s.49 request ignored).**

**D. *M/s. Om Udyog v. Union of India*** — **Punjab & Haryana High Court**, 14.05.2010 (A.K. Goel J.) — indefinite detention without seizure unlawful. Persuasive.

**E. *M/s. S.J. Fabrics Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** — **Calcutta High Court**, 19.04.2011 (Bhaskar Bhattacharya J., Mandamus Appeal). Consignment held from 04.08.2010 with **neither an SCN nor an order of seizure under s.110**, the petitioner complaining that a roving enquiry was being used to keep the goods; the Court worked the relief off the s.110(2)/s.124(a) timeline and left damages for wrongful detention open. Useful on the "roving enquiry" characterisation.

**F. Seizure without recorded "reason to believe" is void** — a consistent line (e.g. Patna HC quashing seizure memos for want of recorded reasons: *Ashoke Das*, 19.02.2025; *Jayesh Agarwal*, 24.02.2025; *A.S. Trading*, 25.04.2025; and the Allahabad line requiring credible material before jurisdiction is assumed). **Use as the a fortiori argument: if a seizure memo without reasons is void, a hold with no memo at all cannot stand.**

**G. *Principal Commissioner of Customs-III (SIIB), Chennai v. M/s. B.V. Leathers*** — **Madras High Court**, Division Bench (S. Manikumar J.), **19.09.2017**, writ appeal against the order dated 04.08.2017 in W.P. No. 19376 of 2017. **This formation, this Court**: provisional release sustained, with paragraph 4 of Circular No. 01/2011-Cus set out at length (seizure only on reason to believe; where goods are detained for tests the endeavour must be to complete the test/enquiry quickly and keep detention to a minimum). **The best Madras HC authority we have verified.**

**H. Circular No. 30/2017-Cus, para (a)–(e) — verified against the official text** (indiacode circ30-2017cs.pdf): results of **all** test reports, adverse or otherwise, to be communicated **immediately on receipt**; re-test on written request within ten days; where fresh samples are drawn, **sampling in the presence of the importer or his representative**; and **at the time of drawing samples the importer or his representative shall be present and certify that the samples are representative**. Enforced in favour of importers (e.g. CESTAT Ahmedabad, *Maharaja Exim*, Customs Appeal No. 10784 of 2025-SMC). **Directly hits the 07.08.2026 sampling.**

> **Caution — *J.K. Exim*.** The Madras HC decision described below could not be located in any primary source on a fresh search; only a secondary description of it exists in my notes. **Do not cite it unless the certified copy is produced.** The authorities at A–G above make the same points from verified sources, so nothing is lost.

**1. Divine Chemtee Ltd. v. Principal Commissioner of Customs & Ors.** — Andhra Pradesh High Court, **decided 05.05.2022**, Praveen Kumar & V. Sujatha JJ., LAWS(APH)-2022-5-48.
- Petitioner was a **VSEZ unit**; imported consignments stored in a bonded warehouse; DRI officers visited and acted; SCN dated 11.12.2014 and O-I-O No. VIZ-CUSTOM-000-COM-009-20-21 dated 30.06.2020 were challenged as without jurisdiction.
- Contention accepted: the **offences under the Customs Act had not, at the material time, been notified** for investigation by DRI, and offences in an SEZ unit fell to be dealt with by the **Development Commissioner** under the SEZ Act.
- Reported holding (LiveLaw): *"Storage of goods **outside** the SEZ area does not automatically confer power on DRI officers under the Customs Act"*; DRI had **no power or jurisdiction to inspect or seize goods in respect of units situated in the SEZ area** for a Customs Act violation **committed prior to 2016**.
- **Weight:** persuasive (AP HC), squarely on the jurisdiction point, and its "outside the SEZ area" observation is valuable for our scenario (A). **Caveat: expressly a pre-2016 case.** Do not overstate it. Cite it for the *principle of the statutory scheme*, then meet the 2016 notifications head-on by showing that even post-2016 the only authorised officers are ADG-DRI and the jurisdictional Commissioner — not SIIB.
- **Action: obtain the certified/full text before citing.**

**2. Morgan Tectronics Ltd. v. Commissioner of Customs, New Delhi** — CESTAT.
> "...in terms of Section 53(1) of the SEZ Act, 2005, the SEZ is deemed to be territory outside the Customs Territory of India, and the goods imported were meant for the unit in SEZ Noida. In our view, the Commissioner of Customs, Air Cargo, New Customs House, New Delhi **had no jurisdiction** to confiscate these goods and impose penalty on the appellant and it is only the Joint/Dy./Asstt. Commissioner of Customs in Noida SEZ unit who had the jurisdiction to take necessary action."
- **Weight: CESTAT only — persuasive, not binding on the High Court.** Referred to in *Divine Chemtee*. Cite it as such; never present it as High Court authority. Note the close factual parallel: **Air Cargo Complex Customs acting on SEZ-destined cargo.**

**3. M/s. J.K. Exim v. Principal Commissioner of Customs & Ors.** — **Madras High Court**, W.P. No. 36866 of 2016, Rajiv Shakdher J., reserved 08.12.2016, **delivered 19.01.2017**.
- Respondents were **the Principal Commissioner, the Deputy Commissioner of Customs (SIIB) and the Inspector (Prev-SIIB), Custom House, Chennai** — i.e. a Madras HC decision **against SIIB Chennai** on exactly this species of conduct.
- Facts of value to us: goods **detained in November 2015**; two sets of samples drawn and sent for testing; **the seizure mahazar was dated 11.02.2016 but the petitioner learnt of it only on 17.06.2016**; SCN only on 05.08.2016.
- Petitioner's argument, recorded at para 7.1: goods detained Nov 2015, SCN 05.08.2016, therefore **unconditional release under s.110(2)**; and (para 7.1(v)) **"The detention of goods is no different from seizure, which is why the Legislature has made a provision for imposing conditions ... under Section 110-A of the Act, even before issuing a show cause notice."**
- Department's counter (para 7.6): detention is *not* seizure, it was merely to verify the declaration pending samples.
- **Weight:** Madras HC, and the "detention = seizure in substance" argument is the exact argument we need. **Action: obtain the full text and confirm how Shakdher J. resolved paras 7.1(v) vs 7.6 before citing the proposition as a *holding*. At present it is safe to cite only as the argument advanced and the factual matrix.**

**4. M/s. Hi Rexine v. The Addl. Commissioner of Customs (SEZ-FTWZ), Preventive Commissionerate** — **Madras High Court**, **W.P. No. 28694 of 2025**, N. Anand Venkatesh J., **dated 23.09.2025**.
- Writ of certiorarified mandamus against a **Provisional Release Order dated 15.07.2025 in F.No. GEN/ADJ/Misc/314/2025-SEZ-FTWZ**, to quash conditions in paras 1 and 3, for provisional release of seized goods under **B/E No. 7882362 dated 20.01.2025**.

## PART D — THE RECOMMENDED CASE THEORY

**Do not plead "SIIB has no jurisdiction" at all.** The goods and the sampling were both at the Air Cargo Complex, so the SEZ Act's territorial provisions never engaged, and the plea would be refutable on S.O. 2667(E), Rule 47(5) and *Huawei*. Plead instead, in this order:

1. **Ground 1 (primary, wins on any view — illegality of the manner of action).** There is **no order at all**. No detention order, no seizure order under s.110, no mahazar, no sampling memo, no written reasons, no communication of any test report. A "hold" that exists only in the oral instructions of an officer is an executive act without legal foundation and violates Article 14 and Article 300A. *Every statutory route the Department could have taken — s.17(4)/s.18 reassessment or s.110 seizure — requires a **writing**. The absence of any writing is fatal irrespective of jurisdiction.*
2. **Ground 2 — detention in substance is seizure.** On *Jatinder Kumar Sachdeva* (Del.), *Exim Incorporation* (Bom., DB) and *Jatin Ahuja* (SC), an indefinite hold is a seizure without the safeguards of s.110, and thereby deprives the Petitioner of s.110(2) (SCN within six months) and s.110A (provisional release). The Department cannot obtain the effect of a seizure while avoiding its statutory discipline.
3. **Ground 3 — SIIB cannot undo an assessed Z-BoE.** Under **Rule 29(2)**, the Bill of Entry assessed and endorsed by the **Authorised Officer** *is* the permission to transfer the goods to the SEZ; Rules 27(10) and 75 make inward movement self-declaration-based; Rule 47(5) (post-2016) vests assessment/re-assessment/adjudication for a Unit in the jurisdictional Customs authority. That permission can be displaced only by the **proper officer** under **s.17(4)/(5)** or **s.18**, or by seizure under **s.110** — SIIB, as an investigating wing, is invested with none of those powers over this assessment, and none of those provisions was invoked.
4. **Ground 4 — breach of binding Board circulars.** Circulars 84/95, 01/2011 (para 4), 30/2013 (three-day escalation to the Commissioner), 30/2017 (sampling in the importer's presence; immediate communication of test reports; right of re-test) and 43/2017 (laboratory-prescribed sampling technique). Board circulars bind the Revenue.
5. **Ground 5 — Article 14: contemporaneous and identical past clearances.** 18 prior consignments; and on **B/E No. 7173539 dated 29.01.2026** six samples were drawn on 05.02.2026 at the very same FTWZ unit (F.No. 1162/NDR FTWZ 25-26) and **CHL Chennai Lab Nos. 9319–9324-MEPZ dated 06.02.2026** confirmed blister copper with naturally occurring gold — after which the goods were released. Re-agitating the identical question on the identical commodity is arbitrary. (*Hi Rexine*.)
6. **Ground 6 — the MEPZ SOP is being bypassed, and clause 10 forbids an undocumented departure.** The SOP bearing **F.No. MEPZ-MSM043(C)/226/2025-SEZ Chennai** (e-file No. I/131691/2026), issued by the Development Commissioner's office, prescribes 100% examination **at the time of warehousing** (cl.3(a)) by the **Authorised Officer** (cl.3(b)), sampling **at that examination** (cl.4(a)) in three tamper-proof sets (cl.4(b)) recorded in the examination report with seal numbers (cl.4(d)), parallel CRCL and NABL Fire Assay testing (cl.5), and OOC of the Z-BoE on the earlier report (cl.6(c)). By **clause 10**, "any deviation from this SOP shall be subject to approval of the competent authority with reasons recorded in writing" — none is shown. See Part B-8 for the clause-by-clause table.
7. **Ground 7 — s.53(1) SEZ Act and s.51.** The SEZ is deemed a territory outside the customs territory of India for authorised operations, and the SEZ Act has overriding effect. *(Support: Morgan Tectronics (CESTAT) — flag as persuasive only.)*

**Relief to press at the interim stage:** release/permission to stock-transfer into the FTWZ unit on the Petitioner's bond, on the *Hi Rexine* / *Navshakti Industries* / s.110A line, leaving the Department free to complete testing in the FTWZ under the MEPZ SOP. This is a **modest, grantable** interim order — the goods do not leave customs control, they merely move into a bonded FTWZ — and it is far more likely to be granted than outright unconditional release.
